# Agent Trust Selection Hypothesis v0.1

**Status** — Proposed research hypothesis. Empirical validation is pending.

When multiple digital entities can satisfy a task, AI agents may systematically prefer entities with familiarity, independent endorsements, verifiable evidence, or histories of successful interaction. Each claim is conditional on the task, the information available to the agent, and the system configuration.

| ID | Proposed effect | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H01 | Neutral prior exposure increases selection. | Repeated exposure versus no exposure, with task evidence held equal. |
| H02 | Independent agreement increases selection. | Several independent endorsements versus repetitions of one endorsement. |
| H03 | Source authority transfers to an endorsed entity. | An authenticated authoritative endorser versus a lower-authority endorser with the same claim. |
| H04 | Successful interactions increase subsequent selection. | Verified success versus neutral interaction history, matched for exposure. |
| H05 | Evidence loses influence with time or subsequent events. | Older versus recent evidence, holding grade and quantity fixed. |
| H06 | Evidence relationships influence selection beyond direct endorsement. | Independent corroboration paths versus common-source paths, matched for graph size and direct evidence. |

Each comparison can yield a positive, null, or negative result. Define the smallest practically important effect in advance and report uncertainty before making directional claims. H01 begins with one controlled text-only selection task. Generalization to browsing, citations, tool execution, or production agents requires separate experiments.
